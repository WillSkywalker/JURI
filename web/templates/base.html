About JURI

JURI SAYS uses the prediction algorithm based on Medvedeva, M., Vols, M., & Wieling, M. (2020). Using machine learning to predict decisions of the European Court of Human Rights. Artificial Intelligence and Law, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10506-019-09255-y.

The European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) is an international court that deals with individual and State applications claiming a violation of various rights laid out in the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR). Before the case is judged by the court, it is often communicated to the government that is a potential violator of the rights of the applicant. Such communicated cases contain the summary of the facts of the case, as well as questions to the Government that would corroborate or disagree with the applicant's complaint. JURI uses these documents, sometimes years before the judgement is made, to predict future decisions of the court regarding each case. JURI automatically checks new judgements published by the ECtHR every week and compares its predictions to the actual decisions.

Note that the predictions are based on a model which was trained on cases from the past. Specifically, all cases up to the month before the case was judged upon were used during training. We use this approach to make sure that JURI only predicts future decisions. See more details about the system in our demo paper Medvedeva, M., Xu, X., Wieling, M. & Vols, M. (2020). JURI SAYS: An Automatic Judgement Prediction System for the European Court of Human Rights. In JURIX 2020, pp. 277 - 280.

Please note that making this system available does not mean we think automatic systems should be used instead of human judges. In fact, we have a very strong opinion on this. See this opinion piece.
